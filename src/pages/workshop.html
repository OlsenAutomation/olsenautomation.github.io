What you’ll practice

One useful process—not fifty magic prompts.

Bring an ordinary problem. We’ll work through it together using a five-move loop you can repeat after the workshop.

01Ask

Start with what you are honestly trying to accomplish.

02Add

Give it the missing context, constraints, notes, or photograph.

03Inspect

Look for assumptions, missing facts, and confident-sounding errors.

04Correct

Tell it what missed the point and continue the conversation.

05Check

Verify anything important before you rely on it or send it.

Learning aims for the first workshop

  • Start a useful AI conversation without memorizing a formula.
  • Use rough notes or a photograph to add context.
  • Ask follow-up questions instead of accepting the first answer.
  • Recognize when an answer needs an independent check.
  • Complete one personally useful task with guidance.

This is not…

  • A coding boot camp.
  • A lecture about robots replacing everyone.
  • A sales pitch for automating your whole life.
  • A room where everyone else already knows more than you.
  • A promise that AI is always correct.

A real project

The physical world gets the final vote.

I had an idea for a mechanical whale that could print as a flat kit. AI helped me move from description to geometry. Printing and assembling it showed what was actually wrong—and gave us the information needed for the next version. Project account and testing observations are owner-reported.

“AI helped me attempt it. Testing told me whether it actually worked.”

You will not be expected to build this in the beginner workshop. The latest v0.5 build adds a display platform and nameplate. Adjusting wave spacing restored movement, while clip retention still needs work. See the latest build, photos and testing notes.

  1. Get comfortable with the tool

    Open the app, use text or voice, and understand what AI is—and is not.

  2. Improve a vague request

    Watch an ordinary question become useful through context and follow-up.

    Turn messy fictional notes into an organized plan without using sensitive information.

    Apply the five-move process to one thing you would genuinely like help with.

I’m Brian Olsen. I did not begin as a traditional software engineer. I began with problems I wanted to solve and things I wanted to make.

With AI, I’ve built working software, games, physical electronics, 3D-printed mechanisms, visual-production workflows, and systems for evaluating information.

I’ve also watched AI misunderstand instructions, invent details, lose context, produce attractive errors, and pass automated tests while still failing in the real world.

This workshop comes from both sides of that experience: what AI makes possible, and why your judgment still matters.
